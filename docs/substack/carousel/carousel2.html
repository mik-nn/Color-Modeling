The footnote

The Other 12%

Part 1 reached 88–90% of same-mode substrate pairs. But a stubborn minority fails no matter how many patches you measure. Two things are true of them:

Not random noise

The failures cluster in a consistent location with a repeatable, measurable signature.

Not an anchor problem

Best-case placement (a GA optimized on the failing pairs) recovers about half, then plateaus.

◆ Two distinct physical causes — and both are print-setup problems, not model limits.

Finding 1

It's Chromatic,
Not Lightness

Split the worst errors (ΔE₀₀ > 3) into lightness, chroma and hue. On Canon, 99% are chroma-dominated:

Chroma & hue outweigh lightness by nearly 5×. Epson shows the same pattern — and it all lives at the highest ink coverage.
◆ A per-ink hue/chroma twist at the gamut edge — exactly what a smooth multiplicative model can't express.

Mechanism A

Over-Inking:
Chroma Rolls Off

Past a peak t*, more ink subtracts chroma and rotates hue. 23 of 24 papers do it:

t* is an automatic perceptual ink limit, read straight from spectra — no target densitometry.
◆ Clipping at t* costs ~5% of shadow volume and 0% of the chroma boundary. Gamut barely notices.

04
Mechanism B

Ink Holdout:
A Different Beast

DecorMatte, a matte canvas, is the single paper that doesn't fold — yet it's the hardest to predict. Ink sits on the surface instead of absorbing:

Chroma still rises to full ink (no fold to clip) — so this is not an ink-limit problem. It's a media mismatch, fixed by the right media preset. Fold and holdout are independent (correlation 0.03).
◆ Two mechanisms at the edge, two different cures — an ink cap here would do nothing.

The payoff

The Error Is
a Mode Sensor

The error-blind ink limit lands on the failure region — 70% of the worst patches sit above it. Excluding them, against a matched random control:

Random exclusion buys +0.3 pp; dropping the over-limit patches buys +8 pp. The limit is on the reference profile — usable up front, not in hindsight.
